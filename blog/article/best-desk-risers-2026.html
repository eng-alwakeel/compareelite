Best Desk Risers 2026

Desk risers elevate your monitor to eye level to prevent neck and shoulder strain during long work sessions. We tested the 6 best desk risers of 2026.

By Sarah Mitchell ·May 8, 2026

A monitor positioned at the wrong height is one of the most common causes of neck pain and shoulder tension for office workers. When your screen is too low, you're forced to tilt your head down repeatedly throughout the day — a position that puts enormous stress on the cervical spine. A desk riser elevates your monitor to eye level, allowing you to maintain a neutral head position and reducing the cumulative strain that leads to chronic neck and shoulder pain. Desk risers serve a dual purpose: ergonomic improvement and additional storage. Most designs include a raised platform for monitors or laptops with a lower shelf for keyboard, mouse, or other accessories, effectively turning a flat desk surface into a two-tier workspace. This is particularly valuable in smaller home offices where maximizing vertical space helps keep the desktop organized. We tested desk risers for stability with heavy dual-monitor setups, surface quality, cable management features, aesthetic design, assembly ease, and practical storage utility. Whether you're setting up a home office or improving an existing workstation, here are the six best desk risers for 2026.

Buying Guide

Finding the Right Height

The optimal monitor height places your screen so the top of the display is at or slightly below eye level, allowing your eyes to look slightly downward at the center of the screen. This is the natural resting position for your eyes and head and minimizes neck strain. Most desk risers elevate surfaces by 4-6 inches, which is appropriate for most desk heights and chair setups. To calculate your ideal riser height: sit in your normal working position, measure from your desk surface to your eye level, then subtract the height of your monitor. If this calculation gives a negative number, your monitor is already at or above eye level and you may not need a riser. People who use external keyboards with a laptop need higher elevation (8-12 inches) to bring the laptop screen to eye level.

Single vs. Dual Monitor Support

If you use dual monitors, choose a riser with sufficient width to support both displays and enough weight capacity for both screens. Most dual monitors together weigh 15-30 lbs — ensure the riser is rated for this weight with appropriate safety margin. Wide risers (36-48 inches) accommodate two monitors side by side, while narrower risers (24-32 inches) may require placing monitors at an angle or using monitor arms for the second screen. Some specialty dual-monitor risers include a center notch or adjustable placement for flexible positioning. If you anticipate future monitor upgrades, choose a riser rated well above your current needs.

Storage and Organization Features

Desk risers typically include a lower shelf for keyboard and mouse storage when not in use (particularly useful with standing desk converters), side compartments for phones, pens, and small accessories, and built-in cable management holes. The best risers integrate these features without cluttering the aesthetic. If you have many accessories, look for risers with multiple compartments or dedicated phone holder slots. A riser with a lower shelf at keyboard height creates a natural work surface where the keyboard slides under the monitor when video calls require a cleaner background. Consider whether your accessories include a full-size keyboard plus mouse — both need to fit in the lower shelf simultaneously for this to work effectively.

Material and Construction Quality

Desk risers come in three primary materials: steel/metal (most durable, supports heaviest loads), bamboo (natural aesthetic, moderate durability), and MDF/wood composite (affordable, variable quality). Steel risers are the most stable for heavy dual-monitor setups and environments where the desk may be bumped. Bamboo risers offer a natural look that suits home office aesthetics but should be kept dry — moisture can cause warping. MDF/wood composite risers are affordable and varied in quality — check that edges are smooth and well-finished and that the surface coating won't peel with normal use. Non-slip rubber or silicone feet prevent the riser from sliding on smooth desk surfaces.

Cable Management

Cable management is one of the most valuable features of a well-designed desk riser. Look for built-in cable routing holes or slots that let you run monitor, keyboard, and peripheral cables from the back to the front of the riser without visible tangling. Some risers include a rear cable channel that hides cables completely. Even a simple cutout in the back panel significantly reduces cable clutter. Combine a riser with cable clips or Velcro straps to fully organize your workspace. For standing desk users who use a desk riser as a monitor platform, cable flexibility matters more — cables need enough slack to accommodate height changes without tension.

Stability and Weight Capacity

A riser must be stable enough to hold monitors without risk of tipping. Look for weight ratings well above your actual monitor weight — if your monitors weigh 20 lbs combined, choose a riser rated for 40-50 lbs. Wide-base designs (risers with legs or platforms extending forward) are more stable than narrow A-frame designs. For heavy curved ultrawide monitors or dual setups, metal risers with solid platform bases are the safest choice. Rubber feet prevent the riser from sliding on the desk. Test the stability of your setup by gently pushing the monitor side-to-side once mounted — any significant wobble indicates insufficient stability for the weight, and you should consider a monitor arm instead of or in addition to the riser.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a desk riser better than a monitor arm?

Desk risers and monitor arms solve the ergonomic height problem differently and suit different setups. A desk riser elevates your entire monitor (or multiple monitors) to a fixed height and adds storage below — it's simple, stable, and includes organizational features. A monitor arm clamps to the desk and allows infinite height adjustment, tilt, swivel, and even rotation between landscape and portrait orientation — maximum flexibility for one or two monitors, ideal for standing desk users who frequently change height. For static setups with a fixed height and multiple monitors, a desk riser is often the better choice. For dynamic setups (standing desks, single-monitor users who frequently reposition, or users who need precise height fine-tuning), a monitor arm offers capabilities a riser can't match.

How high should my monitor be?

The ergonomically correct monitor height places the top edge of the screen at or slightly below your eye level when sitting with correct posture. Your eyes should naturally look slightly downward (5-15 degrees) to the center of the screen. This minimizes neck flexion and reduces strain on the cervical spine. If your monitor is too low, you'll bend your neck forward constantly; too high, and you'll extend your neck upward, which also causes strain. For most people, the screen center should be about 2-3 inches below eye level. If you wear bifocals or progressive lenses, you may need the screen slightly lower to use the correct portion of the lens. When in doubt, sit in your working chair, look straight ahead, and place the monitor so its center is within 1-2 inches below that sightline.

Can a desk riser work with a standing desk?

Yes, but with considerations. If your standing desk already adjusts to the right height for your body, a riser may not be necessary — the desk itself provides the height adjustment. However, if you use a standing desk at a fixed height (many budget models only adjust in a few positions), or if your standing desk can't reach the right height for your monitor, a riser can supplement the desk height adjustment. For sit-stand desks that go very high, ensure the combined height of desk plus riser doesn't push the monitor above your eye level when standing. Some users prefer a riser on a standing desk specifically for additional storage, even when the height adjustment isn't needed.

How do I reduce cable clutter with a desk riser?

A desk riser helps organize cables by routing them through built-in management holes and keeping the desk surface cleaner. For comprehensive cable management: run monitor cables through the riser's cable holes from rear to front, then use Velcro cable ties or adhesive cable clips along the underside of the riser platform to bundle remaining cables neatly. A cable management box (a covered box that sits behind the riser to contain power strips and excess cable length) eliminates the last visible cable mess. Wireless keyboards and mice eliminate those cables entirely — a significant improvement if you currently have keyboard and mouse cables crossing the desk. For the cleanest aesthetic, consider routing power cables down through a cable grommet in the desk surface to a power strip mounted below the desk.

What weight can a desk riser support?

Most desk risers support between 20-50 lbs, sufficient for one to three monitors. A typical 24-27 inch monitor weighs 8-15 lbs; a 32-inch monitor weighs 12-20 lbs; a 34-inch ultrawide weighs 16-25 lbs. For dual monitor setups, add both weights to check against the riser's capacity. Choose a riser rated for 150-200% of your actual load for a safety margin — a 30 lb load should use a riser rated for at least 45-50 lbs. Metal (steel) risers typically have the highest weight ratings (40-100 lbs). Bamboo risers support 30-50 lbs. MDF and composite wood risers vary from 20-40 lbs. Never stack monitors in ways that concentrate weight at the edge of the platform, which can create tipping hazards even within the stated weight limit.

How important is ergonomics when choosing home office equipment?

Ergonomics is the most important factor for home office equipment used for 4 or more hours per day, as discomfort and poor posture accumulate into musculoskeletal problems over months and years. OSHA and Mayo Clinic ergonomic guidelines identify the chair and desk height relationship as the most critical factor — forearms should be parallel to the floor when typing, with feet flat on the floor or a footrest. Monitor height should position the top of the screen at eye level or slightly below to prevent neck flexion. Investing in ergonomically sound primary equipment (chair, desk, monitor position) provides a higher return on health and productivity than any other home office upgrade.

What is the best way to set up a home office for productivity?

An effective home office setup prioritizes visual ergonomics, audio quality for calls, and lighting that minimizes eye strain. Position the primary monitor directly in front of you at arm's length, with the top of the screen at or slightly below eye level. Place task lighting to the left or right of the monitor (never behind or in front) to prevent glare and reflections. Use a dedicated headset or microphone and camera for video calls rather than laptop built-ins to project a professional presence. Separate your workspace visually from living areas when possible — a dedicated room significantly improves focus compared to working from a couch or dining table, even if only separated by a room divider.
