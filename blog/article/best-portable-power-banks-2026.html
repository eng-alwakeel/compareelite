Buying Guide

Total Capacity and Charging Cycles

Rated mAh capacity is measured at 3.7V internally, but your device charges at 5V — meaning actual usable capacity is typically 60-70% of the advertised number after voltage conversion losses. A 26,800mAh bank like the Anker 737 delivers roughly 16,000-18,000mAh of usable output — enough for 3-4 full iPhone charges. Most quality cells are rated for 500 charging cycles before capacity drops below 80%. The Anker 737 and Baseus 65W use high-grade lithium-ion cells tested to industry standards. Avoid no-name brands that overstate capacity with no third-party verification.

Fast Charging Output Wattage

Output wattage determines how quickly your device charges. Standard USB-A delivers 5W — too slow for most use cases. USB-C Power Delivery starts at 18W for phones and goes up to 140W for laptops. The Anker 737 Power Bank tops this guide at 140W USB-C output, enough to charge a MacBook Pro at near-wall speed. The Baseus 65W delivers 65W PD — sufficient for most ultrabooks and all smartphones. The Anker Nano Power Bank 10K outputs 30W, ideal for fast-charging phones. The INIU Power Bank delivers 22.5W — fast enough for phones but insufficient for laptops.

Number of Ports and Cable Types

Multiple ports let you charge several devices simultaneously, but total output wattage is shared across all active ports. Look for at least one high-wattage USB-C PD port plus a USB-A port for legacy cables. The Baseus 65W Power Bank 20K leads this guide with 4 ports — two USB-C and two USB-A — making it the best option for charging a laptop, phone, and earbuds simultaneously. The Anker 737 features two USB-C PD ports and one USB-A. The Anker Nano Power Bank 10K has just one port but includes a built-in USB-C cable, eliminating cable dependency for phone users.

Size and Weight for Travel

Power bank size is a direct tradeoff against capacity. Jacket-pocket portability requires under 200g and a slim form factor. The Anker Nano Power Bank 10K is the smallest in this guide at roughly credit-card thickness and under 180g — it disappears into any bag. The INIU Power Bank 22.5W is similarly slim at 10,000mAh. The Baseus 65W at 20,000mAh weighs around 450g — manageable in a backpack but too heavy for a pocket. The Anker 737 at 26,800mAh weighs 680g (1.5 lbs) and requires dedicated bag space. Match capacity to your actual carry habits, not your worst-case scenario.

Passthrough Charging Support

Passthrough charging lets you plug the power bank into a wall outlet while it simultaneously charges your connected devices. This is essential for desk setups and travel where outlet access is limited. The Anker 737 Power Bank supports passthrough charging at full output speed — plug into the wall and keep charging your laptop and phone without swapping cables. The Baseus 65W Power Bank 20K also supports passthrough. The Anker Nano Power Bank 10K and INIU Power Bank 22.5W have limited or no passthrough support. If you plan to use your power bank as a desktop charging hub, verify passthrough before buying.

Safety Certifications

Lithium-ion batteries carry genuine fire and explosion risks if built to poor standards. Look for UL certification (US safety standard), CE marking (EU), and built-in protections: overcharge protection, short-circuit protection, temperature control, and over-voltage cutoff. The Anker 737 carries UL certification and Anker's proprietary PowerIQ 4.0 safety technology, which monitors charging in real time. The Baseus 65W and INIU Power Bank both carry CE and RoHS certifications. Avoid power banks from unknown brands lacking any third-party certification — the cost savings are not worth the risk of a defective cell.

How long do portable power banks last before needing replacement?

Most lithium-ion power bank cells are rated for 500 charge cycles before dropping below 80% of original capacity.
