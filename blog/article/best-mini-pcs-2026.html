Best Mini PCs 2026

Mini PCs pack desktop-class performance into palm-sized form factors perfect for home offices, media centers, and light workstations. Here are the 6 best in 2026.

By Sarah Mitchell ·May 8, 2026

Sarah Mitchell is a consumer tech reviewer with 8 years of hands-on testing experience. She has evaluated over 400 products for leading publications and specializes in home office ergonomics and productivity gear.

Mini PCs have evolved from underpowered curiosities into legitimate desktop alternatives capable of handling office productivity, content creation, home theater duty, and even light gaming — all in enclosures small enough to mount behind a monitor or tuck into a media cabinet. Brands like Beelink, MINISFORUM, Intel (NUC), and ASUS have made compact computing more compelling than ever with the latest AMD Ryzen and Intel Core processors packed into passively or efficiently cooled chassis. The mini PC market in 2026 is defined by a few key trends. AMD Ryzen 7000 series chips have brought significantly improved integrated graphics performance that closes the gap with entry-level discrete GPUs for light gaming and video editing workflows. Intel's 12th and 13th Generation processors bring excellent efficiency with performance hybrid architectures that handle multi-threaded workloads well in fanless or low-noise thermal envelopes. Thunderbolt 4 and USB4 connectivity enables external GPU docks for users who occasionally need discrete GPU power without buying a dedicated gaming tower. RAM and storage upgradeability — historically a weakness of compact PCs — has improved with most reputable brands now shipping socketed SO-DIMM DDR5 slots and standard M.2 NVMe storage. We evaluated these mini PCs across productivity workflows, media playback, gaming benchmarks, and thermal management to compile this ranking of the six best mini PCs available in 2026.

Buying Guide

Intel vs. AMD Mini PC Processors

The processor choice between Intel and AMD defines a mini PC's performance character, particularly for tasks involving integrated graphics. AMD's Ryzen 7000 series (and the Ryzen 6000 series before it) ships with Radeon 680M and 780M integrated graphics that significantly outperform Intel's Iris Xe in gaming and GPU-accelerated workloads. A MINISFORUM with a Ryzen 9 6900HX can run titles like Elden Ring or Cyberpunk 2077 at 1080p low settings — a feat beyond what Intel integrated graphics can accomplish. Intel's advantages lie in Thunderbolt 4 connectivity (AMD equivalents use USB4 which is similar but not identical), vPro enterprise manageability features on Pro models, and strong single-core performance that benefits office productivity and web browsing workloads. For pure productivity use — Office, web browsing, video conferencing, light photo editing — both Intel and AMD mini PCs perform comparably. For gaming, light 3D rendering, or any GPU-accelerated task, AMD Ryzen mini PCs hold a meaningful integrated graphics advantage.

RAM and Storage Upgradeability

Mini PC RAM and storage upgradeability varies significantly between models and is a crucial factor for long-term value. Mini PCs using SO-DIMM DDR4 or DDR5 slots allow RAM replacement and upgrades by simply removing the bottom panel and swapping modules — a 5-minute process with a screwdriver. LPDDR5X memory is soldered directly to the motherboard and cannot be upgraded, making it critical to purchase the right amount at the time of purchase. Standard M.2 NVMe 2280 slots accept any off-the-shelf SSD, allowing inexpensive storage expansion — a 2TB NVMe SSD costs under $100 and doubles or quadruples the factory storage. Some models include both an M.2 slot and a 2.5-inch SATA bay for large storage configurations suitable for Plex media servers. Before purchasing any mini PC, verify whether RAM is socketed or soldered, how many M.2 slots are included, and whether a SATA bay is available. These specifications significantly affect the long-term cost of ownership and the PC's ability to meet growing storage needs without replacement.

Display Output Capabilities

Mini PCs typically offer 2-4 video outputs through combinations of HDMI, DisplayPort, USB-C DisplayPort Alt Mode, and Thunderbolt. The exact combination determines how many monitors you can drive and at what resolution and refresh rate. Most mini PCs support dual 4K@60Hz simultaneously through two HDMI ports — sufficient for a dual-monitor home office setup. Premium mini PCs with Thunderbolt 4 or USB4 support triple or quad 4K displays through a combination of native ports and Thunderbolt hubs. Refresh rate capability matters for gaming and motion-sensitive work: most mini PCs support 4K@60Hz natively, while USB4/Thunderbolt models can support 4K@120Hz or 144Hz displays. If you plan to use a 144Hz gaming monitor with your mini PC, verify the specific port and maximum refresh rate specification — HDMI 2.0 is limited to 144Hz at 1080p, while HDMI 2.1 and DisplayPort 1.4 support 4K@144Hz. Most budget mini PCs include HDMI 2.0 rather than 2.1, limiting high refresh rate options.

Thermal Performance and Fan Noise

Mini PCs face a fundamental thermal challenge: high-performance processors in small enclosures generate heat that must be managed with limited airflow. The approach varies significantly across the market. Completely fanless mini PCs (like those using Intel N100 or N5105 processors) are totally silent and use the case itself as a heatsink — they're ideal for living rooms, bedrooms, and shared office environments but cannot sustain high processor frequencies for extended periods. Actively cooled mini PCs with fans run quietly at idle but audibly during sustained workloads — quality brands like Beelink and MINISFORUM use high-quality fan bearings that stay quiet under typical office loads. Under extended video rendering or compilation workloads, cheaper mini PCs can thermal throttle, reducing clock speeds to protect the hardware. Before purchasing a performance mini PC, check independent reviews that test sustained workloads (not just burst performance) — a Cinebench multi-thread test run for 10+ minutes reveals whether the cooling system can maintain performance.

Connectivity: Ports, Wi-Fi, and Ethernet

Mini PCs offer a range of connectivity options that affect their usefulness across different workflows. USB port selection matters for connecting keyboards, mice, external drives, and other peripherals — look for at least 4 USB-A ports total, with at least 2 being USB 3.2 Gen 2 for fast external storage transfers. A USB-C port with DisplayPort Alt Mode or Thunderbolt is increasingly important for modern dock compatibility. Networking is critical: a 2.5 Gigabit ethernet port versus a standard 1 Gigabit port matters for home network attached storage access, local backup speeds, and Plex media server serving — the 2.5G advantage is real when transferring large files over a home network with 2.5G infrastructure. Wi-Fi 6 (802.11ax) is the current standard for mini PCs — Wi-Fi 6E adds 6 GHz band support for faster wireless speeds in congested environments. For desktop use, a wired ethernet connection always outperforms Wi-Fi for stability and throughput. Verify whether the mini PC you're considering includes a 2.5G ethernet adapter or only a standard 1G port before purchasing.

Use Case Matching: Which Mini PC for Which Task

Matching a mini PC to its intended use case prevents both overspending and underpowering. For basic office use — web browsing, Microsoft Office, video conferencing, and light media playback — an Intel N100 or similar efficiency-class processor (Beelink EQ12, Minisforum UN100) handles all tasks well at low cost and in complete silence. For home media server and Plex duties, a Ryzen 5 or Ryzen 7 mini PC with multiple storage bays and a 2.5G ethernet port maximizes library storage and transcode capability. For content creation — photo editing in Lightroom, video editing in DaVinci Resolve, 3D modeling — AMD Ryzen 7 or 9 mini PCs with Radeon integrated graphics provide GPU acceleration that meaningfully reduces render times versus Intel integrated graphics. For casual gaming — older titles at 1080p, indie games, emulation — AMD Ryzen 6000 and 7000 mini PCs with Radeon 680M or 780M graphics deliver playable frame rates in a wide range of titles. For heavy gaming, a mini PC with an external GPU dock is the only path to discrete GPU performance without a traditional tower.

Operating System Options

Most mini PCs ship with Windows 11 Home pre-installed, which is appropriate for general home and office use. However, the compact form factor and typically upgradeable storage make mini PCs popular candidates for alternative operating system configurations. Linux distributions including Ubuntu, Fedora, and Pop!_OS install readily on most mini PCs — driver support for AMD Ryzen and Intel processors is excellent in modern Linux kernels. Linux is particularly suited to mini PCs used as home servers, development environments, or NAS devices where Windows licensing cost and overhead are unnecessary. Chrome OS Flex from Google can be installed on compatible mini PC hardware to create a lightweight, fast Chromebook-like experience focused on web applications. For users building a home lab, a mini PC running Proxmox VE or VMware ESXi hosts multiple virtual machines efficiently given the low power consumption versus a traditional tower server. The ability to replace or dual-boot the operating system is only possible if the mini PC's storage is replaceable — another reason socketed M.2 storage is preferable to soldered eMMC or fixed SSD configurations.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a mini PC replace a traditional desktop computer?

For most home and office users, a modern mini PC can fully replace a traditional desktop tower. The use cases where a mini PC excels include web browsing, email, Microsoft 365 productivity, video conferencing, streaming media playback, and light photo editing — tasks that collectively describe 90% of home computer use. Mini PCs with AMD Ryzen 7 or 9 processors even handle more demanding tasks like video editing in DaVinci Resolve, 3D printing slicer software, music production in a DAW, and casual gaming at 1080p on older or less demanding titles. Where traditional desktops maintain a clear advantage is in heavy gaming requiring a discrete GPU (RTX 4070 and above), workstation-class content creation with GPU rendering (requiring an RTX or Quadro card), and applications that benefit from PCIe expansion cards like video capture or high-channel audio interfaces. For the majority of users who aren't doing sustained GPU-heavy work or gaming, a mini PC with 16-32GB RAM and a quality NVMe SSD outperforms many mid-range desktop towers of 2-3 years ago while consuming a fraction of the power and making no perceptible noise.

How much RAM do I need in a mini PC?

RAM requirements for a mini PC depend on the workload, but general guidance is to start at 16GB and only go lower if budget is extremely constrained. Windows 11 itself requires 4GB to run, but with background processes and a browser with a dozen tabs open, 8GB of RAM shows clear performance limitations — slower multitasking, more reliance on slower page file storage, and occasional stuttering when switching between applications. 16GB of RAM is the practical minimum for comfortable Windows 11 use in 2026 — it handles office productivity, multiple browser windows, video conferencing, and light creative work simultaneously without performance issues. 32GB of RAM benefits users who run multiple heavy applications simultaneously, use virtual machines, work with large datasets, or do photo and video editing with high-resolution files. For purely office and web use, 16GB is genuinely sufficient and spending on more RAM is better redirected to faster storage or a more capable processor. If the mini PC you're considering uses socketed SO-DIMM rather than soldered LPDDR, 16GB is a safe starting point that you can upgrade if your workflow demands it — a future 32GB upgrade costs around $60-80 for compatible modules.

Are mini PCs good for working from home?

Mini PCs are an excellent choice for work-from-home setups, offering several specific advantages over both laptops and traditional desktop towers for home office environments. The compact footprint keeps desk space clear for monitors, notebooks, and other work essentials without the bulk of a tower. A quality mini PC produces minimal fan noise during typical office workloads — far quieter than gaming laptops or full-size desktops under comparable use — an important consideration for home office environments where conference calls and focused work require a quiet space. Most mini PCs support dual 4K monitor output, enabling the productivity-boosting dual-screen setup that remote workers benefit from. The total system cost for a mini PC plus two monitors plus keyboard and mouse typically comes in lower than a comparable laptop configuration that offers similar multi-monitor capability. For IT-managed corporate environments, Intel NUC Pro models with vPro offer remote management capability that enterprise IT departments can manage similarly to traditional desktops. The main limitation for work-from-home use is portability — a mini PC lives on your desk and doesn't travel with you, which matters for workers who split time between home and office.

What is the difference between barebones and pre-built mini PCs?

Barebones mini PCs are sold as kits containing the chassis, motherboard, processor, and cooling system — but without RAM or storage, which you purchase and install separately. The Intel NUC line has historically sold primarily as barebones kits, allowing buyers to choose their own RAM speed and capacity, SSD brand and size, and operating system. Pre-built mini PCs from Beelink, MINISFORUM, and others come fully configured with RAM, SSD, and Windows 11 pre-installed, ready to use out of the box. Barebones kits offer flexibility and can reduce total cost if you already own compatible RAM and storage from a previous build, or if you want to optimize component selection. Pre-built mini PCs cost more but eliminate the assembly and configuration steps, making them accessible to users who aren't comfortable upgrading PC components. For most buyers, a pre-built mini PC with socketed RAM (so you can upgrade later) and a standard M.2 slot offers the best balance — you get immediate usability while retaining the option to expand as needs grow. Verify RAM and storage type before purchasing a pre-built to ensure components are industry-standard replaceable parts rather than proprietary configurations.

Can mini PCs handle gaming?

Mini PCs can handle gaming, but the experience varies significantly based on the game, settings, and the specific processor inside the mini PC. Mini PCs with AMD Ryzen 6000 or 7000 series processors (which include Radeon 680M or 780M integrated graphics) are the strongest performers for gaming without discrete GPU — the Radeon 780M integrated in Ryzen 7040 series chips delivers performance roughly equivalent to a desktop GTX 1050, enabling 1080p gameplay in many older and less demanding titles at medium settings. Games like Fortnite, Rocket League, Minecraft, CS2, Stardew Valley, and most indie titles run smoothly. Modern AAA titles like Cyberpunk 2077, Alan Wake 2, or Hogwarts Legacy require reduced resolution and low settings to achieve playable frame rates. Mini PCs with Intel integrated graphics (Iris Xe) perform notably worse in games — roughly half the gaming frame rate of AMD Radeon 780M in GPU-limited scenarios. For users who want occasional gaming alongside productivity work, an AMD Ryzen mini PC is a capable compromise. For serious gaming, a mini PC paired with an external GPU dock (Thunderbolt 4 or USB4 required) brings discrete GPU performance into a compact package, though the total cost rivals a mid-range gaming laptop.

How long should a quality product in this category last?

Quality products in this category typically provide 5 to 8 years of reliable service with proper care, though software support and feature obsolescence often make users replace them in 3 to 5 years. Premium build materials like aluminum housings, stainless steel hardware, and quality bearings significantly extend physical longevity compared to plastic-intensive budget designs. Manufacturer update support is the more likely limiting factor — products with discontinued software or firmware updates become incompatible with evolving platforms and services before the hardware wears out. Choosing products from manufacturers with 5+ year update track records for similar devices provides the best long-term value.

What warranty should I expect and what does it cover?

Standard manufacturer warranties for consumer electronics typically cover defects in materials and workmanship for 1 year (US standard) or 2 years (EU standard). Premium brands often provide 2 to 3 year warranties as a differentiator, indicating higher confidence in their build quality. Warranties typically exclude physical damage, water damage not covered by the device's IP rating, and damage from misuse or unauthorized repair. Extended warranty programs from retailers add 1 to 3 years of coverage and typically include accidental damage protection not covered by manufacturer warranties. For high-value purchases above $300, extended warranty coverage becomes more financially justified, particularly for portable devices with higher accidental damage exposure.

Business users who require Intel-certified compatibility with enterprise software and priority support should choose the Intel NUC 13 Pro.
