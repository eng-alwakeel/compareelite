Buying Guide

Full-Motion Arm vs. Fixed Riser for Your Workflow

The choice between a full-motion monitor arm and a fixed-height riser depends entirely on how often you reposition your screen. Fixed risers — simple platforms that elevate a monitor by 4 to 6 inches — are rigid but stable, cost under $30, and require no installation. They work well for users who set up once and never move their monitor. Full-motion arms, starting at $35, allow height, tilt, depth, and rotation adjustments in seconds and are essential for sitting and standing desk users who shift posture throughout the day. If you currently work at a fixed-height desk and use a separate keyboard tray, a basic riser may be all you need — but any standing desk user should prioritize a gas-spring arm.

Monitor Weight and VESA Compatibility Check

Before purchasing any monitor stand or arm, verify two specifications: your monitor's weight without its base, and its VESA mounting pattern. Most modern monitors between 24 and 32 inches use VESA 100x100 or 75x75 patterns — both of which are supported by every arm in this guide. Monitor weight without the base typically ranges from 8 to 22 pounds. The Ergotron LX handles up to 25 pounds, while budget options like the North Bayou and HUANUO cap at 17.6 pounds per arm. Ultrawide monitors above 34 inches frequently exceed 20 pounds — always verify weight against the arm's rated capacity before ordering to avoid returns.

Gas-Spring Mechanism vs. Spring Tension Quality

Not all gas-spring mechanisms are equal. Premium arms like the Ergotron LX use Constant Force technology that maintains precise positioning without drift for years, while budget gas springs can lose pressure and allow downward creep within 6 to 12 months of daily adjustment. Signs of a quality gas spring include smooth single-finger movement with no jerk or sticking at any point in the range, adjustable tension via a knob or hex screw that accommodates different monitor weights, and a rated load range of at least 4 to 20 pounds. If you plan to keep the arm for more than 3 years, the premium mechanism in the Ergotron LX is worth the $80 price difference over budget alternatives.

Desk Clamp vs. Grommet Mounting Options

Most monitor arms offer two mounting options: a C-clamp that attaches to the desk edge without drilling, or a grommet mount that passes a bolt through a pre-drilled hole in the desk surface for a more permanent, lower-profile installation. The C-clamp is the default for most users and works on desks from 0.4 to 3.5 inches thick — always verify your desk thickness before ordering. Grommet mounts provide a more stable base for heavier monitors above 15 pounds because the force is distributed through the desk rather than applied to the edge, reducing the risk of surface damage over time. For glass desks or desks with thin edges, a grommet mount is the safer choice.

Cable Management and Desk Aesthetics

A monitor arm without proper cable management creates a tangled bundle of power and display cables that undermines the clean aesthetic the arm was purchased to achieve. Premium arms like the Ergotron LX route cables through an enclosed internal channel from the base post to the monitor mount — these cables are invisible from every angle. Mid-range arms use open clips along the arm surface to bundle cables, which is neater than no management but not fully concealed. Budget arms like the VIVO single mount provide no dedicated cable routing, requiring zip ties or cable sleeves purchased separately. If desk aesthetics matter to your setup, factor cable management quality into your purchase decision before defaulting to the lowest-price option.

Single vs. Dual Monitor Setup Considerations

Single monitor arms start at $35 and are ideal for focused workstations, creative setups with one large display, or desks where a second monitor is not planned. Dual monitor arms range from $59 for budget stacked or side-by-side configurations to $269 for premium independent-arm stacking solutions. The key difference between cheap and expensive dual arms is independent tension calibration — premium options like the Ergotron LX Dual allow each arm to be adjusted for monitors of different weights, which is critical if your primary and secondary displays are different sizes. For users running two identical 27-inch monitors, the HUANUO dual arm at $59 provides excellent value. For mixed-size setups, invest in a dual arm with per-arm tension control.

What is the best monitor stand in 2026?

The Ergotron LX Desk Monitor Arm is the best monitor stand in 2026, earning a 9.7/10 in our testing. What should I look for when buying a monitor stand?

The three most important factors are monitor weight compatibility, adjustment mechanism quality, and mounting type. First, verify your monitor's weight without its base against the stand's rated capacity — most budget arms cap at 17.6 pounds, while premium options handle up to 25 pounds. Second, choose a gas-spring mechanism over a fixed shaft for any standing desk or frequently adjusted setup — it saves significant time daily. Third, decide between a C-clamp and grommet mount based on your desk material and edge thickness. Also check VESA compatibility — most monitors use 75x75 or 100x100 patterns, both of which every arm in this guide supports.

Is a monitor arm worth it over a fixed riser?

For standing desk users or anyone who adjusts posture throughout the day, a full-motion monitor arm is absolutely worth the investment over a fixed riser. Being able to raise your screen by 5 to 6 inches when standing and lower it precisely when seated — in under 3 seconds with a gas-spring arm — directly improves ergonomic alignment and reduces neck strain. The Ergotron LX at $159 pays for itself within weeks if it eliminates the end-of-day neck tension from a fixed-height monitor. For users with a fixed-height desk who never plan to use a standing position, a $25 to $40 fixed riser is a perfectly reasonable choice that still improves ergonomics over having the monitor flat on the desk.

How long does a monitor arm typically last?

A premium monitor arm from Ergotron is built to last 10 or more years — the company backs its products with a 10-year warranty, which is the longest in the category. The gas-spring mechanism is the primary wear component, and in premium arms it maintains its tension for at least 5 to 7 years of daily adjustment. Budget arms from VIVO and HUANUO typically retain full functionality for 2 to 4 years before gas-spring pressure diminishes or plastic components begin to show wear. The steel frame and VESA mount bracket on any arm in this guide should outlast the monitor it supports by several years if the arm is not dropped or overloaded.

Can a monitor stand work with an ultrawide monitor?

Yes, but ultrawide monitors require careful weight verification before purchasing a stand. Most 34-inch ultrawides weigh between 18 and 24 pounds without their base, which exceeds the rated capacity of budget arms like the North Bayou and HUANUO at 17.6 pounds per arm. The Ergotron LX handles up to 25 pounds and is the safest choice for ultrawides up to 34 inches. For 38-inch or larger ultrawides that exceed 25 pounds, look for heavy-duty arms rated at 30 pounds or more — these are a niche category not covered in this guide. Always confirm the VESA pattern on your specific ultrawide model before ordering, as some manufacturers use non-standard 200x100 patterns.

How important is ergonomics when choosing home office equipment?

Ergonomics is the most important factor for home office equipment used for 4 or more hours per day, as discomfort and poor posture accumulate into musculoskeletal problems over months and years. OSHA and Mayo Clinic ergonomic guidelines identify the chair and desk height relationship as the most critical factor — forearms should be parallel to the floor when typing, with feet flat on the floor or a footrest. Monitor height should position the top of the screen at eye level or slightly below to prevent neck flexion. Investing in ergonomically sound primary equipment (chair, desk, monitor position) provides a higher return on health and productivity than any other home office upgrade.

What is the best way to set up a home office for productivity?

An effective home office setup prioritizes visual ergonomics, audio quality for calls, and lighting that minimizes eye strain. Position the primary monitor directly in front of you at arm's length, with the top of the screen at or slightly below eye level. Place task lighting to the left or right of the monitor (never behind or in front) to prevent glare and reflections. Use a dedicated headset or microphone and camera for video calls rather than laptop built-ins to project a professional presence. Separate your workspace visually from living areas when possible — a dedicated room significantly improves focus compared to working from a couch or dining table, even if only separated by a room divider.
