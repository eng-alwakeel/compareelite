Buying Guide

Weight Capacity and Monitor Size Compatibility

Every monitor arm lists a maximum weight and VESA hole pattern it supports. Most monitors use the standard 75x75mm or 100x100mm VESA pattern, but verify yours before buying. Arms rated for 17 to 22 pounds cover the vast majority of 24 to 27-inch monitors. If you own a 32-inch or larger monitor, or an ultrawide curved display, target arms rated for 25 pounds or more like the Ergotron HX, which supports up to 42 pounds. Buying an arm at its weight limit shortens lifespan and increases drift.

Range of Motion and Adjustment Flexibility

A monitor arm should allow you to tilt the screen at least 20 degrees forward and backward, swivel left and right by at least 90 degrees, and rotate to portrait orientation. Vertical height adjustment of 13 inches or more is essential if you switch between sitting and standing. The Ergotron LX provides 360-degree rotation at the monitor head and a 13-inch height travel range, which covers standing desk transitions cleanly. Arms with fewer than 90 degrees of swivel limit your ability to share the screen with someone beside you.

Desk Clamp vs Grommet Mount

Most monitor arms attach via a C-clamp on the desk edge or a grommet bolt through a pre-drilled hole in the desk surface. C-clamps work on desks up to 3.5 inches thick with a flat edge. Grommet mounts provide a more stable, permanent connection for heavy monitors and require a 1.5 to 2.5-inch hole in the desk. If your desk has a reinforced lip, beveled edge, or glass top, a grommet mount is the safer choice. The VIVO and WALI arms include both mount types in the box; Ergotron arms typically ship with one type.

Cable Management Features

Good cable management channels route power and display cables from the monitor head down through the arm and out at the base, keeping your desk surface clean. Enclosed channels like those on the Ergotron LX hide cables completely, while open channels on budget arms like the WALI require supplemental cable ties. Count how many cables your monitor needs to route: a USB-C monitor with power delivery may only need one cable, while a monitor with a power brick and a separate video cable needs space for two. Enclosed management is worth paying extra for if desk aesthetics matter to you.

Arm Mechanism: Spring vs Gas vs Friction

Monitor arms use three movement mechanisms. Friction arms use a tightened bolt to hold position and require tool adjustment to change tension as the monitor or your preferences change. Spring arms use a coiled spring for counterbalance and hold lighter monitors well but may drift over years of use. Gas spring arms like the Flexispot MA01 use a sealed gas cylinder for smooth, tool-free adjustment with 17 pounds of counterbalance force. Gas springs are the premium choice for desks where you frequently reposition throughout the day, while friction arms work fine for set-and-forget monitor positions.

Build Quality and Warranty

Metal construction at the pivot joints and arm segments determines how much the arm flexes under load and how long it lasts. Budget arms under $40 typically use plastic pivot caps over steel bolts, introducing 2 to 4mm of flex at full extension under a 15-pound monitor. Premium arms like the Ergotron LX and HX use full aluminum construction with zero measurable flex in our testing. Ergotron backs all its arms with a lifetime warranty, while most budget brands offer 1 to 2 years. For a product you will use daily for 5 to 10 years, warranty length directly affects total cost of ownership.

What should I look for when buying a monitor arm?

Start with weight capacity: your arm must be rated for at least 2 pounds more than your monitor to prevent drift. Next, confirm VESA compatibility, since most monitors use 75x75mm or 100x100mm patterns. Verify your desk thickness fits the clamp range, typically 0.4 to 3.5 inches for C-clamps. Finally, consider range of motion: look for at least 90 degrees of swivel, 20 degrees of tilt, and 13 inches of vertical travel if you use a standing desk. The Ergotron LX covers all these criteria at its $159 price point.

Are cheap monitor arms worth it or should I spend more?

Budget arms under $40 like the WALI at $28 are worthwhile if your monitor weighs under 12 pounds and you rarely reposition it. They support the basic VESA standard and provide a clean desk setup at minimal cost. Spending more buys you heavier weight capacity, smoother gas spring adjustment, enclosed cable management, and longer warranties. If you adjust your monitor multiple times per day or own a monitor heavier than 15 pounds, the Ergotron LX at $159 pays for itself in ergonomics and durability within the first year.

How long does a monitor arm typically last?

A quality monitor arm with metal construction should last 8 to 15 years under daily use. Ergotron backs its arms with a lifetime warranty and tests pivot mechanisms for over 20,000 adjustment cycles. Budget arms with plastic pivot caps typically show joint wear after 2 to 4 years of frequent repositioning. The main wear points are the pivot bolts and spring or gas cylinder. Regularly checking bolt tension and keeping the pivot joints clean of dust extends arm lifespan significantly regardless of the price tier.

Can I use a monitor arm on a glass desk?

Yes, but you need a grommet mount rather than a C-clamp for glass desks. C-clamps require gripping the desk edge with pressure that can crack glass over time. A grommet mount threads through a pre-drilled hole in the glass and distributes weight evenly with a backing plate, making it safe for tempered glass desks rated for the arm's weight. If your glass desk has no pre-drilled hole, contact a local glass shop about drilling a 2-inch grommet hole, which typically costs $20 to $40 and is safer than attempting it yourself.

How important is ergonomics when choosing home office equipment?

Ergonomics is the most important factor for home office equipment used for 4 or more hours per day, as discomfort and poor posture accumulate into musculoskeletal problems over months and years. OSHA and Mayo Clinic ergonomic guidelines identify the chair and desk height relationship as the most critical factor — forearms should be parallel to the floor when typing, with feet flat on the floor or a footrest. Monitor height should position the top of the screen at eye level or slightly below to prevent neck flexion. Investing in ergonomically sound primary equipment (chair, desk, monitor position) provides a higher return on health and productivity than any other home office upgrade.

What is the best way to set up a home office for productivity?

An effective home office setup prioritizes visual ergonomics, audio quality for calls, and lighting that minimizes eye strain. Position the primary monitor directly in front of you at arm's length, with the top of the screen at or slightly below eye level. Place task lighting to the left or right of the monitor (never behind or in front) to prevent glare and reflections. Use a dedicated headset or microphone and camera for video calls rather than laptop built-ins to project a professional presence. Separate your workspace visually from living areas when possible — a dedicated room significantly improves focus compared to working from a couch or dining table, even if only separated by a room divider.
