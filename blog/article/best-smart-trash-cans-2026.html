Best Smart Trash Cans 2026: Tested & Ranked

Smart trash cans tested and ranked for 2026: the EKO Mirage-T leads at $89.99, with picks for odor control, big kitchens, and tight bathrooms.

By Alex Rivera ·June 28, 2026 ·11 min read

Alex Rivera is a smart home specialist and IoT consultant who has reviewed over 500 connected devices and contributed to leading consumer technology outlets.

Reviewed by Mike Chen, Senior Product Analyst

Smart trash cans have moved from novelty to a genuine kitchen upgrade, and for 2026 the category is dominated by motion-sensor models that open hands-free the moment you wave a hand near the lid. We tested six of the most popular touchless cans across kitchens, bathrooms, and home offices to see which ones detect reliably, seal in odor, and survive months of daily cycling without a dead motor or a sticky lid. The field splits into two camps. Full-size 50-liter (13-gallon) cans like the EKO Mirage-T, the iTouchless 13 Gallon, and the two Ninestars models handle main-kitchen duty, while the 80-liter EKO EcoSmart targets large families and the slim 10-liter EKO Astro slips into a bathroom or beside a desk. Prices in this round ran from $49.30 to $99.99, and the gap between the best and worst lid response was wide enough to notice every single day. We scored each can on sensor accuracy, lid speed, odor sealing, battery endurance, and build quality before checking prices, so the rankings reward performance rather than marketing. Below you will find the full ranked list, a buying guide that explains what actually matters, and answers to the questions shoppers ask most about automatic sensor bins.

Buying Guide

How Motion-Sensor Trash Cans Detect Your Hand

Every can in this guide uses an infrared sensor mounted in the lid that fires when it sees an object move within range. Two numbers decide how natural the can feels: trigger distance and response time. The best models, like the EKO Mirage-T, react within roughly 0.3 seconds at about 6 inches, so the lid is open before your hand arrives. Cheaper units such as the Ninestars DZT-50-28 top out near 4 inches, which means you have to reach closer. Also check the hold-open window: the Ninestars DZT-50-9BK auto-closes after about 5 seconds, while several EKO models stay open longer for slow scraping. False triggers matter too. A well-tuned sensor ignores a passing pet or a person walking by, so look for adaptive sensing in the product description. If a can sits in a high-traffic doorway, prioritize a tighter detection cone over a wide one to avoid the lid flapping open all day.

Choosing the Right Capacity for Your Kitchen or Bathroom

Capacity is the single biggest decision, and it is measured in both liters and gallons. A standard family kitchen wants 45 to 50 liters (12 to 13 gallons), which is exactly what the EKO Mirage-T, the iTouchless 13 Gallon, and both Ninestars cans deliver. Households of five or more, or anyone who cooks in bulk, should step up to the 80-liter (21.1-gallon) EKO EcoSmart so the bin does not overflow mid-week. Bathrooms, home offices, and bedrooms need far less: the 10-liter (2.6-gallon) EKO Astro is sized for tissues and small waste and stands only about 8 inches wide. Remember that bigger cans take standard bag sizes too, so a 50-liter can uses common 13-gallon liners. Measure the gap where the can will live, including overhead clearance, because oval cans like the iTouchless need about 4 inches above the lid to open. Match capacity to how often you want to take out the trash.

Battery Life vs AC Adapter Power Options

Automatic cans draw power to run the sensor and motor, and there are two camps. Battery-only cans are the simplest to place anywhere: the EKO Mirage-T runs about 8 months on 4 AA cells, while the iTouchless 13 Gallon stretches to roughly 1.5 years on 4 larger D batteries. Cans that accept an AC adapter, like the iTouchless, remove battery anxiety entirely if you have an outlet nearby, which suits a fixed kitchen spot. Factor the running cost of replacement batteries into the price: D cells cost more than AA cells but last far longer, so the total spend can even out. If you choose a battery model for a kitchen, keep a spare set on hand, because a dead battery turns a touchless can into a fixed lid until you swap them. For bathrooms, the smaller EKO Astro sips power and rarely needs attention given its lighter daily use.

Odor Control: Carbon Filters, Gaskets, and Lid Seals

Smell is the complaint that sends people back to a step can, so odor control deserves attention. There are three approaches. Activated-carbon filters, used by the iTouchless 13 Gallon, sit in the lid and adsorb gases; iTouchless rates one cartridge for about 3 months, after which you buy replacements. Silicone or rubber gaskets, like the rim seal on the Ninestars DZT-50-28, physically clamp the lid down to trap air inside the can. Tight-fitting lids without a seal, common on the Ninestars Original, rely on a snug fit alone and let more smell escape. For a kitchen that handles meat, fish, or diapers, a model with both a gasket and a carbon filter performs best. Whatever you pick, empty perishable waste promptly and rinse the inner bucket weekly, because no filter overcomes rotting food left for days. Removable inner liners, found on the EKO EcoSmart, make that cleaning far quicker.

Build Quality and Fingerprint-Resistant Finishes

A trash can lives in a damp, high-use corner, so materials decide how it looks after a year. Stainless steel is the standard, but the grade and coating vary. Fingerprint-resistant finishes, found on the EKO Mirage-T and the black Ninestars DZT-50-9BK, shrug off the smudges that plague bright polished stainless near a sink. A removable inner bucket, like the one in the EKO EcoSmart, lets you carry just the liner to the curb and rinse the inside without tipping the whole can. Check the lid mechanism too: heavier-gauge hinges, as on the EKO models, survive thousands of cycles, while lighter hardware on budget cans can loosen. Splash resistance matters in bathrooms, where the EKO Astro uses a sealed shell that wipes down without rusting. Inspect how the bag is hidden as well, since a retaining ring keeps the liner tucked out of sight and stops it from sliding into the can when full.

Standard Bags vs Proprietary Refill Systems

Bag compatibility is a long-term cost you should weigh before buying. Every can ranked here, from the iTouchless 13 Gallon to the EKO Astro, accepts standard off-the-shelf trash bags, so you can buy 13-gallon kitchen liners or small bathroom bags in bulk for a few cents each. Some self-sealing smart cans on the market instead use proprietary refill rings that automatically seal and swap the bag, which is convenient but locks you into branded refills that can add $60 to $140 a year. For most households, a sensor can that takes standard bags is the more economical choice, and you keep the freedom to use scented or heavy-duty liners. If you do want a self-sealing can, budget for the refills up front and confirm the rings are in stock, because a self-sealing model is useless without them. Bag-retaining rings, found on both Ninestars cans, grip a normal liner so it does not slip even without a proprietary system.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best smart trash can in 2026?

The EKO Mirage-T 50L is our best overall smart trash can for 2026 at $89.99. It pairs a fast infrared lid that opens in about 0.3 seconds with a roomy 50-liter (13.2-gallon) rectangular body that sits flush against a wall at only 11 inches deep. It runs roughly 8 months on 4 AA batteries and its fingerprint-resistant brushed coating wipes clean in one pass. If odor is your main worry, the iTouchless 13 Gallon at $74.99 adds an AbsorbX activated-carbon filter rated for about 3 months per cartridge. Large households should look at the 80-liter EKO EcoSmart at $99.99, while a bathroom or office is better served by the slim 10-liter EKO Astro at $49.30. Across all six cans we tested, the Mirage-T struck the best balance of sensor speed, capacity, build quality, and price, which is why it earned the top 9.5/10 score.

Are motion-sensor trash cans worth it over a step pedal can?

Motion-sensor trash cans are worth it if you cook often or share a kitchen, because the touch-free lid keeps raw-food bacteria off your hands and off the can itself. The best models in our test, like the EKO Mirage-T, open within about 0.3 seconds of a wave, so there is no waiting and no pedal to step on while your hands are full of groceries. The trade-offs are power and price: a sensor can needs batteries or an AC adapter, whereas a step can needs neither, and you typically pay $20 to $40 more for the automation. A pedal can has fewer parts to fail, but the EKO and iTouchless motors we tested are rated for repeated daily cycling. If hygiene and convenience rank high for you, the roughly $74.99 to $99.99 spent on a quality sensor can is justified; if you want the simplest possible bin, a manual step can still does the job.

What is the best budget smart trash can for a kitchen?

For a full-size kitchen on a tight budget, the Ninestars DZT-50-28 is the strongest pick at $58.75, which is the lowest price of any 13-gallon (50-liter) can in our test. It undercuts the EKO Mirage-T by about $31 yet still delivers touch-free opening, a slow-close lid that lowers over roughly 3 seconds, and an odor-sealing rim gasket that the pricier Ninestars Original lacks. If your space is smaller, the 10-liter EKO Astro costs even less at $49.30 and suits a bathroom or office, though its 2.6-gallon capacity fills within a day or two in a busy household. Either way, both cans accept standard off-the-shelf trash bags, so you avoid the $60-to-$140 yearly cost of proprietary refill rings used by some self-sealing models. For most shoppers who want a real kitchen bin without overspending, the $58.75 Ninestars DZT-50-28 is the value leader here.

How long do the batteries last in an automatic trash can?

Battery life depends on the cell type and how often the lid cycles, but our picks span a wide range. The iTouchless 13 Gallon lasts the longest at up to about 1.5 years on 4 D batteries, because D cells hold far more charge than smaller sizes. The EKO Mirage-T runs roughly 8 months on 4 AA batteries, which is typical for AA-powered cans. Heavy kitchen use with dozens of openings a day shortens those figures, while a bathroom can like the 10-liter EKO Astro lasts longer thanks to lighter traffic. If you dislike swapping batteries, choose a model such as the iTouchless that also accepts an AC adapter and place it near an outlet for unlimited run time. Keep a spare set of the correct size on hand, because a dead battery leaves the lid stuck closed until you replace it. Expect to budget a few dollars a year for replacement cells.

Do smart trash cans work with standard kitchen trash bags?

Yes, every can we ranked works with standard off-the-shelf trash bags, so you are not locked into branded refills. The 50-liter models, including the EKO Mirage-T, the iTouchless 13 Gallon, and both Ninestars cans, take common 13-gallon tall kitchen liners that cost a few cents each in bulk. The 10-liter EKO Astro uses small bathroom-size bags, and the 80-liter EKO EcoSmart fits large 20-to-21-gallon liners. Many of these cans add a bag-retaining ring, found on both Ninestars models, that grips the liner edge so it does not slip down inside when the can is full. The exception in the wider market is self-sealing smart cans that automatically seal and swap the bag using proprietary rings; those require branded refills that can add $60 to $140 a year. None of our six picks use that system, so you keep the freedom to choose scented or heavy-duty liners for about the same low cost.

What size sensor trash can do I need for a family kitchen?

For a typical family kitchen, aim for 45 to 50 liters, which is 12 to 13 gallons, and that is exactly the size of the EKO Mirage-T, the iTouchless 13 Gallon, and both Ninestars cans in our test. A 50-liter can holds a few days of waste for a household of three or four before it needs emptying. If you have five or more people, cook in bulk, or hate taking out the trash often, step up to the 80-liter (21.1-gallon) EKO EcoSmart, which can skip mid-week emptying entirely. Smaller spaces tell a different story: a bathroom, bedroom, or office is better matched to the slim 10-liter (2.6-gallon) EKO Astro, which is only about 8 inches wide. Always measure the floor gap and the overhead clearance before buying, since oval cans like the iTouchless need roughly 4 inches above the lid to open. Match the capacity to how many people you feed and how often you want to empty it.

How do you clean and maintain an automatic sensor trash can?

Keeping an automatic can hygienic takes only a few minutes a week. Empty perishable food waste promptly, then rinse the inner bucket with warm soapy water; cans with a removable liner bucket, like the 80-liter EKO EcoSmart, make this far easier because you lift the bucket out instead of tipping the whole unit. Wipe the stainless shell with a damp cloth, and use a fingerprint-resistant model such as the EKO Mirage-T or the black Ninestars DZT-50-9BK to cut down on visible smudges near the sink. Keep the infrared sensor window clean and dry so it triggers reliably, and avoid spraying water directly into the lid electronics, since only the body is splash-resistant on cans like the EKO Astro. Replace odor filters on schedule, about every 3 months on the iTouchless 13 Gallon, and swap batteries before they die. A monthly deep clean with a diluted vinegar rinse keeps smells down between filter changes.
