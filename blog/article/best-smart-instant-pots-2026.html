Best Smart Instant Pots 2026: Tested & Ranked

Smart Instant Pots do far more than pressure cook. We tested 6 top models on speed, app control, and versatility to find the best multi-cookers for 2026.

The best smart Instant Pots of 2026 go further by adding Wi-Fi or Bluetooth connectivity, companion app control, and guided recipe programs that automatically set time and pressure based on what you're cooking. For busy households, that means dinner with minimal manual setup and no guesswork. All six products are available on Amazon with Prime shipping, and prices reflect current listings as of May 2026. Buying Guide

Quart Capacity and Household Size

Choosing the right capacity is the single most important buying decision for a multi-cooker. The standard 6-quart size — used by the Instant Pot Duo 7-in-1, Ultra 10-in-1, and Pro Plus Wi-Fi — comfortably serves 4–6 people for soups, stews, and braises. A 6-quart pot holds a whole 4–5 lb chicken with room for vegetables and broth. For households of 6 or more, or for batch cooking that requires making 8–10 servings at once, the 8-quart models like the Ninja Foodi 14-in-1 and Instant Pot Duo Crisp are the better choice. The 8-quart capacity fits a 7–8 lb pork shoulder — the maximum size that makes practical sense for most home kitchens. Avoid 3-quart mini models if you cook for more than two people; the restricted fill capacity means batching almost every recipe, defeating the convenience advantage of a multi-cooker. Fill the pot to no more than 2/3 capacity for most foods (and 1/2 capacity for foods that expand, like grains), so a 6-quart pot yields about 4 quarts of usable cooking space.

Pressure Settings and Cook Time Impact

Most multi-cookers operate at high pressure (around 10–15 psi) and low pressure (around 5–6 psi). Higher pressure translates directly into faster cooking: dried chickpeas that take 90 minutes to simmer on the stovetop cook in 35–40 minutes at 15 psi. The Instant Pot Ultra 10-in-1 allows manual pressure adjustment in 1 psi increments, which enables precise control for delicate proteins like fish (best cooked at 5–7 psi) or tough collagen-rich cuts like short ribs (best at 14–15 psi). The Crock-Pot Express maxes at 12 psi, which means approximately 10–15 extra minutes on long-cook recipes compared to 15 psi models. For most everyday cooking — rice, chicken, soups, and steamed vegetables — the 2–3 psi difference between models is inconsequential. The pressure difference matters most for dried legumes, large bone-in cuts, and stocks where you want maximum collagen extraction from bones in minimum time. Always factor in pressure build time (8–15 minutes depending on liquid volume) when planning meals around a scheduled dinner time.

Smart Features and App Connectivity

Wi-Fi connectivity, as found in the Instant Pot Pro Plus, adds genuine convenience for certain households. The ability to start a pressure cook cycle remotely — for example, triggering a chicken soup to begin 20 minutes before arriving home — is a real time saver when your schedule is unpredictable. The companion app's 1,900+ guided recipes that auto-configure the cooker are particularly useful for new pressure cooker owners who aren't comfortable setting pressure levels manually. However, app connectivity requires a 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi network and a smartphone with the Instant Pot app installed. If your household rarely changes plans on short notice, or if everyone who cooks is already comfortable with the manual settings, the $50 premium over the base Duo 7-in-1 may not pay off. For households with multiple cooks of varying comfort levels with pressure cooking, the guided recipe feature alone can justify the upgrade cost. Bluetooth-only models have a range of roughly 30 feet; Wi-Fi models can be controlled from anywhere with cellular service.

Air Frying and Dual-Lid Design

The Ninja Foodi 14-in-1 and Instant Pot Duo Crisp both combine pressure cooking with air frying via an interchangeable lid system. This TenderCrisp or dual-lid approach solves a fundamental limitation of standard multi-cookers: pressure cooking produces moist, tender proteins but cannot achieve the Maillard browning and crisp surface texture that dry-heat methods create. With a dual-lid cooker, you pressure cook a whole chicken for 25 minutes to fully cook through, then switch to the air fry lid for 5–8 minutes at 400°F to crisp the skin to a golden-brown finish — results that would otherwise require two separate appliances. The tradeoff is storage: both lids must be kept accessible, adding roughly 18–22 inches of cabinet or counter storage compared to a single-lid model. The 8-quart size is the standard configuration for dual-lid models, since the larger diameter provides better hot-air circulation in air-fry mode. If counter and cabinet space are limited, a standalone air fryer used alongside a standard Instant Pot can be more practical than a dual-lid unit.

Cleaning and Maintenance

Long-term satisfaction with a multi-cooker depends heavily on how easy it is to clean. The Instant Pot Duo 7-in-1's stainless steel inner pot is fully dishwasher-safe and holds up to daily washing without surface degradation. By contrast, the Instant Pot Duo Crisp's inner pot and air fry basket are hand-wash only — plan for 5–10 extra minutes of cleanup per use. The sealing ring is the most maintenance-intensive component across all models: it absorbs the aromas of strongly flavored foods like garlic, chili, and fish and should be replaced every 12–18 months with daily use. Replacement rings cost $8–$12 each and are widely available on Amazon. Keep a second sealing ring dedicated to desserts and dairy dishes to prevent savory flavor transfer into rice pudding or yogurt. The steam valve and float valve should be rinsed under warm water after every use — food debris in the valve can prevent proper sealing and trigger burn errors during subsequent cooks. Lid condensation cup cleaning takes under 60 seconds and prevents drips onto the counter.

Safety Certifications and Pressure Release

All six models in this roundup carry UL or ETL safety certification and include multiple redundant safety systems: overpressure protection valves, lid locking mechanisms that prevent opening under pressure, automatic temperature limiting, and anti-blockage steam venting. The Instant Pot line includes 10 safety mechanisms in the Duo series, rising to 11 in the Ultra and Pro Plus models. The most important safety distinction is the steam release valve design. The Instant Pot Duo uses a manual twist valve that requires the user to rotate a knob to release steam — this is effective but can result in steam burns if the user reaches above the valve during release. The Crock-Pot Express uses an easy-release button that moves the valve with a single finger press while keeping the hand clear of the steam path, which is meaningfully safer for households with children or elderly users. The Ninja Foodi uses a pressure release button on the lid handle with similar hand-clearance design. Natural pressure release — allowing pressure to drop on its own over 15–25 minutes — is always the safest method and produces the most tender results for meat-based dishes.

Can I use a multi-cooker instead of a slow cooker?

Yes — all six models in this roundup include a dedicated slow cook function that replicates a traditional Crock-Pot's low-and-slow cooking method. The Instant Pot Duo 7-in-1 and Ultra 10-in-1 both offer low (190°F) and high (200°F) slow cook settings, equivalent to the low and high settings on a standalone slow cooker. A 8-hour pot roast recipe designed for a traditional slow cooker transfers directly to these multi-cookers without modification. The key advantage of using a multi-cooker over a dedicated slow cooker is the saute function: you can brown onions, sear beef, and bloom spices directly in the pot before switching to slow cook mode, eliminating the need to dirty a separate skillet. The Crock-Pot Express Multi-Cooker is a particularly natural transition for existing Crock-Pot slow cooker users, as it uses the same basic interface and compatible stoneware inserts. The one functional difference to note is that multi-cooker slow cook lids don't form the same tight steam-sealing fit as traditional slow cooker lids, which can cause slightly faster liquid evaporation over 8–10 hour cook cycles — adding 1/4 to 1/2 cup of extra liquid to long-cook recipes compensates for this difference.

How long does a 6-quart Instant Pot take to come to pressure?

Pressure build time for a 6-quart Instant Pot Duo depends primarily on the volume and temperature of liquid in the pot. With 2 cups of room-temperature liquid and a moderate food load (2–3 lbs of chicken thighs), the Duo reaches full high pressure in approximately 8–10 minutes. With a full 6-quart load of cold liquid straight from the refrigerator, pressure build can take 15–20 minutes. This pre-pressurization time should always be added to the recipe's stated cook time when planning meals: a recipe that calls for 30 minutes of pressure cooking will actually take 38–50 minutes from the time you press Start. The Instant Pot Duo Crisp's 8-quart size takes 12–15 minutes to reach pressure with a standard load due to the larger internal volume. The Ninja Foodi 14-in-1's 8-quart chamber has similar build times. One practical tip: starting the pot with hot tap water (120°F) rather than cold water reduces pressure build time by 3–5 minutes — a meaningful difference when you're cooking on a weeknight deadline. The keep-warm function activates automatically after cooking completes, maintaining food at 145–175°F for up to 10 hours.

What foods should not be cooked in a pressure cooker?

Several food categories perform poorly or unsafely in pressure cookers. Dairy-based dishes — milk, cream, butter, and cheese — should never be pressure cooked, as they foam aggressively under pressure and can block the steam valve, creating a safety hazard. Add dairy only after pressure cooking is complete with the lid off. Delicate seafood like shrimp, scallops, and fish fillets overcook in the 5–10 minute window before pressure builds, resulting in rubbery texture; steam or saute these on the stovetop instead. Thin pasta and noodles absorb so much liquid under pressure that they become a starchy paste — cook pasta in a pot of boiling water separately and add it to finished pressure-cooked sauces. Foods like applesauce, oatmeal, and split peas foam heavily and can block the float valve; always add 1 tablespoon of butter or oil to these recipes and never fill beyond the 1/2 fill line. Breaded or battered foods lose their coating under steam and pressure — the Ninja Foodi 14-in-1 and Instant Pot Duo Crisp's air fry lids are the right tool for these items. Finally, quick-cooking vegetables like spinach, peas, and asparagus are destroyed by pressure cooking; add them in the last 5 minutes of slow cook mode or after pressure has released.

Do smart home devices work without internet?

Many smart home devices require internet connectivity for initial setup and cloud-based features, but local control capability varies significantly by brand and platform. Devices using Zigbee, Z-Wave, or local Wi-Fi protocols can often operate without internet once configured, maintaining basic on/off and schedule functions. Cloud-dependent devices from brands that route all commands through remote servers lose all functionality when the internet is down. Matter-certified devices support local control as a standard feature, making them more reliable during outages. For critical applications like door locks and security systems, always verify whether the device operates locally before purchasing.

Are smart home devices secure?

Smart home device security varies widely and requires active management by the user. Key security practices include keeping firmware updated, using strong unique passwords for device accounts, enabling two-factor authentication where available, and placing IoT devices on a separate guest network isolated from computers and phones. Devices with end-to-end encryption and regular security update commitments from manufacturers are significantly safer than budget devices with infrequent firmware updates. Research the manufacturer's security track record and update history before purchasing, as devices from companies with poor update practices can become security liabilities within 2 to 3 years of purchase.
